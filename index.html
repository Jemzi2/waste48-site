The disposal site records the load. Not the carrier.

Digital waste tracking in England starts with the disposal site. When a load arrives, your site reports what it received and Defra returns a waste tracking ID. That ID is the record.

WhoPermitted waste sites that receive waste — transfer stations, treatment sites, metal recyclers, skip yards, landfill, household waste recycling centres.
WhatFor each load: the carrier, the waste codes, the quantity, and any hazardous or POPs detail — sent to Defra as one receipt.
WhenBy the end of the second working day after the load arrives. Waste48 shows the clock on every unfiled receipt. We call it the 48.
If notNon-reporting is a breach under the Digital Waste Tracking (England) Regulations 2026, with penalties and a public register of sanctions. The duty sits with the site — carriers' own reporting duties don't begin until October 2027.

Two AI assistants. One job each. Neither can do the other's.

They are software, not people — parts of Waste48 that we named so a receipt's history reads plainly: "Reg recorded it, Vera checked and sent it, Dave approved it." Here is what each one does and, just as important, what each one cannot do.

Reg
The recorder · Reg handles the regs
Does
Builds the record for each load as it arrives: carrier registration, waste codes, quantity, hazardous or POPs detail. Scores how complete it is and names what is missing in plain words. Takes typed entries and CSV uploads.
Never
Sends anything to Defra. There is no route from Reg to Defra in the software. Never guesses a value — unknown stays unknown and is flagged. Never edits a record after it has been filed.
Vera
The verifier · from veritas, truth
Does
Checks the completed receipt against Defra's own rules and against sense — code matches description, weight plausible for the container, carrier registered. Sends it inside the window, stores the waste tracking ID that comes back, and keeps checking that everything sent was confirmed.
Never
Creates or changes a load. Sends without your site's approval when human review is on. Sends the same load twice. Marks anything "filed" unless Defra returned an ID.

The questions sites are asking.

What is a waste tracking ID?

The reference Defra issues when a receipt of waste is reported. One per movement. It is the site's proof that it recorded the load, and a carrier can quote it. Waste48 stores it against the load and only ever shows an ID Defra actually returned — "filed" means filed.

Is it really 48 hours?

The rule is the end of the second working day after the load arrives. Over a weekend that is longer than 48 hours; on a Thursday afternoon it can be shorter than you think. Waste48 runs the statutory clock, not a fixed 48 — the name is a reminder, not the rule.

Can I just use Defra's spreadsheet?

Yes. Defra provides a free receipt-of-waste template spreadsheet for sites that don't have API software — you download it, complete the mandatory fields, and upload it; you must use Defra's template, not your own, and a new one for each upload. It is on GOV.UK under Report receipt of waste, section "Spreadsheet".

Where it stops being enough: nothing checks the rows before you upload, nothing tells you which load a rejection refers to, the waste tracking IDs come back separately from your records, and the working-day clock is yours to watch. Waste48 exists for the site that has tried that for a fortnight. If the template suits you, use it — that is an honest answer.

Do I need my weighbridge connected?

No. You need the weight and the reference on the receipt. Type it, or upload the export your weighbridge software already produces. Connecting the weighbridge directly is a later conversation; it is not needed to be compliant.

Why would Defra reject a receipt?

The commonest reasons: no carrier registration number, or hazardous waste with no consignment code. Both may be absent — but only with a reason stated on the receipt. Vera asks for the reason before the receipt can be sent, so the rejection never happens.

Something turned up in a load that wasn't declared. Now what?

Attach it to the load it most likely came from, photograph it, and amend the receipt within the correction window. Waste48 keeps the original and the correction together with the reason. Declaring what you found protects the site; the duty to describe the waste correctly sits with whoever sent it.

Who is liable — the carrier or the site?

From October 2026 the duty to report receipts sits with the receiving site. Carriers' own digital reporting duties begin in October 2027. Until then the site's receipt is the record — and it names the carrier.

Is Waste48 "Defra approved"?

There is no such thing as Defra approval of a provider, and we won't say there is.
